- `losat makeblastdb` and `convert2blastmask` use less memory and time on genomes whose long sequences contain runs of N: the FASTA reader now keeps one residue buffer from record to record instead of growing a new one after every sequence with a gap (D. melanogaster genome: max RSS 105.3 to 74.4 MB and 0.436 to 0.414 s; with `-parse_seqids -mask_data` 116.4 to 82.7 MB and 0.492 to 0.455 s; `convert2blastmask` 67.3 to 36.9 MB and 0.378 to 0.342 s). The buffer stays as large as the longest sequence read so far, so a long sequence with N followed by many short ones takes as much memory as one without N always did (3R then 200,000 short records: 62.3 to 72.1 MB). Databases and masks do not change.
