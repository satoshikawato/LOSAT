# Changelog

All notable release-facing changes and milestones are documented here.

## [Unreleased]

- `tblastn` reads a packed subject's six frames (every `-db` sequence, and `-subject` without `-subject_loc`) from the packed bytes, eight codons from one word, and with AVX2 32 codons at a time (`LOSAT_X_TBNXLATESIMD`, one of the switches above); the frames, and so the output, do not change. One protein against the human genome (`-db`): 0.88 of the time at one thread (NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 takes 1.9 times as long) and 0.94 at 8 threads; the other measured cases 0.99-1.00 ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `blastx -db` checks the residues of each database sequence (no gap character, no byte past NCBIstdaa) in one pass without an early exit, which the compiler vectorizes; the old check stopped at the first bad byte and read one byte at a time. The same sequences are refused with the same messages. One 300-nt query against the first UniRef50 volume (3.0 G residues, one thread, workstation) takes 0.955-0.96 of the time. `blastp -db` checks its blocks in its own pass and is unchanged.
- Added the experimental switch `LOSAT_X_TBXSCANBF` (off unless set; not in the strict set): `tblastx` scans the subject's words against a lookup table without a branch per word (tblastn's loop of `LOSAT_X_TBNSCANBF`, applied to each subject range with NCBI's stops for room), for tables with presence bits in fewer than half of their cells (queries up to about 60 kb); other tables keep NCBI's loop. The output does not change. On one thread the time is 0.72-0.73 of the default for 2-5 kb queries against a 294 kb genome, 0.84 for 3 transcripts (2 kb) against the fruit fly genome database and 0.85 for 10 transcripts (18.8 kb) against the zebrafish cDNA database; the same for queries of 80 kb and more.
- `tblastx` holds each ungapped HSP of its linking list in 88 bytes instead of 152 on 64-bit targets: the record keeps its offsets, sequence lengths, query context, identity count and list positions in 32-bit fields (NCBI BLAST+ keeps them in `Int4`) and the subject frame index in one byte, set only with checked conversions and read back in the types used before, so reports do not change. No switch: a record layout is not chosen at run time. On the workstation (8 cores, 8 rounds) max RSS falls from 158 to 137 MiB on AP027131 against AP027133 (code 4) at 8 threads and from 98 to 92 at 1, and from 217 to 184 MiB (129 to 107 at 1 thread) on NZ_CP006932 against itself; the search takes 0.96-0.99 of the time (merging the seed stage's lists 0.64-0.80, the first linking call's sorts 0.74-0.87).
- `tblastn` links a subject's HSPs testing only the candidates whose subject positions can make an admissible set, in NCBI's order and with NCBI's tests, instead of every HSP in the query-offset window (`LOSAT_X_TBNLINKWIN`, one of the switches above; shadow `LOSAT_X_TBNLINKWINSHADOW`, opt-in). The links and E-values, and so the output, do not change. One protein against the zebrafish genome (`-db`, one thread): 0.60 of the time (24.8 -> 14.8 s; NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 31.3 s); against the human genome 0.87; the fly genome x one batch of proteins at 8 threads 0.76. With the other two switches on: 0.58, 0.82 (0.84 at 8 threads) and 0.81 ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `blastx` finds the subject words that have lookup hits without a branch per word (the loop of `tblastn`'s `LOSAT_X_TBNSCANBF`: the words whose presence bit is set are listed first, then their hits are taken in position order), in its default seed loop and in the fused loop of `LOSAT_X_BXDIAG` (`LOSAT_X_BXSCANBF`, a switch of the strict set; `LOSAT_X_BXSCANBF=0` turns it off). The output does not change. On the workstation (one thread, `-db`, against NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0): a 300-nt fly cDNA piece against the first UniRef50 volume (3.0 G residues; 34 % of the words have hits) takes 0.60 of the time without it and 0.66 of NCBI's time, one 2.1-kb cDNA (84 %) 0.89 and 0.85, and 800 cDNAs against the yeast proteome (98 %) 0.99-1.00.
- `blastx` scans the subject and applies the two-hit diagonal test in one loop and tests the hits of one lookup cell in batches of eight in AVX2 registers (the loop of `LOSAT_X_BPSEED` with the cell test of `LOSAT_X_BPDIAG`), where the lookup table is the standard one (`-word_size 3`, the default), on an x86-64 CPU with AVX2 and for the query batches whose lookup table has at least 5 hits per used cell (`LOSAT_X_BXDIAG`, a switch of the strict set, with `LOSAT_X_BXDIAGMIN` in hundredths: 500; `LOSAT_X_BXDIAG=0` turns it off, and set to a value it runs on any CPU and with `per-hit` or `scalar` cell tests; shadow `LOSAT_X_BXDIAGSHADOW`). A batch with fewer hits per cell is scanned by the direct loop, which is faster there. The output does not change. On the workstation (one thread, `-db`, with `LOSAT_X_BXSCANBF`, against `LOSAT_X_BXDIAG=0`): 800 fly cDNAs against the yeast proteome take 0.71 of the time (about 0.61 of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `-db`), one 2.1-kb cDNA against the first UniRef50 volume 0.93, and a 300-nt piece of it the same time (no batch fused; fusing it took 1.20 times as long).
- `blastx` scans a subject shorter than a word less one letter (one residue with the default word size) from a copy with sentinels after it: with a two-hit window its one scan position reads a word from position 0, which ran past the sentinel (NCBI BLAST+ reads the bytes that follow in its buffer). One scan position extends no hit, so reports do not change.
- Fixed `blastx` at a `-window_size` near 2^29 with two or more subjects (`-subject` or `-db`): its diagonal entries are now NCBI's `DiagStruct` bit-field (a 31-bit `last_hit` and a flag, 4 bytes per entry instead of 8), so the hit offsets of a subject whose diagonal offset passes 2^30 wrap as in NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0, which then lets none of that subject's two-hit seeds through. With a 4,910-residue subject before the subject that has the hits, at `-window_size 536870000`, NCBI reports 3 rows and LOSAT reported 6; both now report 3. At smaller windows the offsets stay below 2^30 and reports do not change. The diagonal table is half its previous size, as large as NCBI's.
- Added the experimental switch `LOSAT_X_TBXLUT` (off unless set; not in the strict set; shadow `LOSAT_X_TBXLUTSHADOW`): TBLASTX builds the full lookup table of a query batch, the table of the reference seed loop, in place from per-cell counts, as BLASTX and BLASTP do by default, instead of copying it out of one list per cell, so reports do not change ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `tblastn` with two or more threads searches the query batches the rule leaves one after another (for example all of them when there are fewer batches than threads) at the same time, each on a pool of its own with its share of the threads, when there are fewer than two subjects per thread (`LOSAT_X_TBNOWNPOOLS`, one of the switches above, in the set with `LOSAT_X_TBNBATCH` and `LOSAT_X_TBNBATCHRULE`; native builds only, since in WebAssembly only the main thread can start threads); reports do not change. Before, they were searched one after another with all threads. One 4.7 Mb genome with 16 query batches at 32 threads: 0.20 of the time ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `tblastn` with two or more threads searches query batches side by side only for the full waves (a multiple of the threads) and only when the subjects hold at most 24 Mb or there are fewer than two subjects per thread (`LOSAT_X_TBNBATCHRULE`, one of the switches above, in the set with `LOSAT_X_TBNBATCH`): the other batches are searched one after another with all threads, as NCBI searches them, so reports do not change. Before, every batch went side by side once there was one per thread. On 28,036 16S subjects (40.6 Mb) with 10 query batches at 8 threads the search takes 0.68 of the time of `LOSAT_X_TBNBATCH` alone, on one 0.6 Mb genome 0.77 ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `blastx` with two or more threads searches the subjects (`-db` or `-subject`) of a query batch it searches as one chunk as a flow of tasks of 64 consecutive subjects that the other threads search ahead, handed to the hit lists in subject order, instead of waves of 64 subjects per thread that wait for their slowest thread (`LOSAT_X_BXSUBJFLOW`, in the strict set from the pull request that added it, 2026-10-17; `=0` returns to the waves; shadow `LOSAT_X_BXSUBJFLOWSHADOW`); reports do not change. Ten transcripts (10.9 kb) against Swiss-Prot `-db`: 16 threads 5.23 to 2.30 s (NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `-db` 2.58 s), 8 threads 7.11 to 3.89 s (NCBI 4.12 s; medians of 3 rounds). With `LOSAT_TIMING`, `blastx_subject_waves` and `blastx_subject_flow` lines ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- `blastx` with two or more threads searches its short query batches as a flow of tasks instead of waves of four batches per thread, writing the reports in input order as they finish, and searches the input's last batches side by side with the whole pool (`LOSAT_X_BXFLOW`, a switch of the strict set, natively; `LOSAT_X_BXFLOW=0` turns it off; the Web reactors keep the waves). The output does not change. Against the yeast proteome at 16 threads it takes 0.71-0.84 of the time of the waves on 800 fly cDNAs and 0.56-0.58 on 50 cDNAs, and the same time on one batch. Timed again as a default (8 threads, with the other blastx defaults): 800 cDNAs take 0.82-0.84 of the time with `LOSAT_X_BXFLOW=0` (0.57-0.62 of NCBI's), 50 cDNAs 0.73-0.74, one batch 1.00; the largest memory is about 5 % higher on 800 cDNAs (70 MB against 67 MB; NCBI 125 MB).
- `tblastn` scans the subject's words against the lookup table without a branch per word (the words whose presence bit is set are listed first, then their hits are copied in order, with NCBI's stop and resume when the offset array is full). The offset pairs, and so the output, do not change (`LOSAT_X_TBNSCANBF`, one of the switches above). One 411-residue protein against the human genome (`-db`): 0.73 of the time at one thread (45.6 -> 33.2 s; NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 44.9 s) and 0.77 at 8 threads; one protein against the zebrafish genome 0.79; batches of many proteins 0.98-0.99 ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- Added `blastx -db NAME`: BLASTX searches an NCBI BLAST protein database (the files of makeblastdb without `-parse_seqids`, or of `LOSAT makedb`; as for BLASTP's `-db`) and gives the output of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `blastx -db NAME -num_threads 1` for outfmt 0, 6 and 7, the same at every `-num_threads`. The database's sequences are read once and kept as stored (about the size of its `.psq` file); the search, the composition-based redo and the reports read the stored residues as NCBI's `-db` does. Added `blastx -dbsize N` for `-db` and `-subject`; `-searchsp`, `-db_soft_mask` and `-db_hard_mask` stay unsupported. A BLASTX search with many subjects no longer keeps a table of queries by subjects per query batch that only its diagnostics read, and the parallel search of a long query's chunks (`LOSAT_X_BXPAR`) keeps only the subjects with hits: a 91 kb query against Swiss-Prot (575,748 proteins) at 8 threads 1,086 to 579 MiB (NCBI BLAST+ 280 MiB; at 1 thread LOSAT 221 MiB). Reports do not change.
- Added `tblastx -db NAME`: TBLASTX searches an NCBI BLAST nucleotide database (the files of makeblastdb without `-parse_seqids`, or of `LOSAT makedb`; format versions 4 and 5, several volumes, alias files as for TBLASTN) and gives the output of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `tblastx -db NAME -num_threads 1` for outfmt 0, 6 and 7, the same at every `-num_threads`. The seed and ungapped stages translate the stored 2-bit bases (where makeblastdb put random bases for ambiguous letters, as NCBI does) and the re-evaluation and the alignments read the stored ambiguity codes; TBLASTX has no shortest-length cutoff, so `-db` and `-subject` of sequences without ambiguous letters give the same hits. The database is read once in blocks of about 1 MiB and held packed (about the size of its `.nsq` file); each thread makes the letters of the sequence it searches. Added `tblastx -dbsize N` for `-db` and `-subject`; `-searchsp`, `-db_soft_mask` and `-db_hard_mask` stay unsupported.
- Added `tblastn -db NAME`: TBLASTN searches an NCBI BLAST nucleotide database (the files of makeblastdb without `-parse_seqids`, or of `LOSAT makedb`; format versions 4 and 5, several volumes, alias files as for BLASTP and BLASTN) and gives the output of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `tblastn -db NAME -num_threads 1` for outfmt 0, 6 and 7, the same at every `-num_threads`. The search translates the stored 2-bit bases (where makeblastdb put random bases for ambiguous letters, as NCBI does) and the alignments and the composition-based redo read the stored ambiguity codes; the hit-saving cutoff uses the database's shortest length (10), so `-db` keeps the weak hits NCBI `-db` keeps and `-subject` does not. The database is read once in blocks of about 1 MiB and held packed (about the size of its `.nsq` file); totals are kept in 64 bits. Added `tblastn -dbsize N` for `-db` and `-subject`; `-searchsp`, `-db_soft_mask` and `-db_hard_mask` stay unsupported.
- `blastn` (all tasks) holds less memory while it searches, with the same output: the query once (one BLASTNA buffer with sentinels, as NCBI BLAST+ holds it, instead of about seven copies, with the four-base bytes of the ungapped extension computed where they are read), the final HSP lists at their exact size, and no growth slack in FASTA records of 1 MiB or more (all programs). Max RSS, same-run measurements: megablast of Sakai (5.5 Mb) against MG1655 at 8 threads 197 to 146 MiB (NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `-db` 153 MiB); 100 16S queries against 28,036 16S sequences at one thread 135.5 to 109.1 MiB.
- Added `blastn -db NAME` for megablast, blastn and dc-megablast: BLASTN searches an NCBI BLAST nucleotide database (the files of makeblastdb without `-parse_seqids`, or of `LOSAT makedb`; format versions 4 and 5, several volumes, alias files as for BLASTP) and gives the output of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `blastn -db NAME -num_threads 1` for outfmt 0, 6 and 7, the same at every `-num_threads`. The search reads the stored 2-bit bases (where makeblastdb put random bases for ambiguous letters, as NCBI does) and rebuilds the ambiguity codes for the alignments, so `-db` and `-subject` of the same sequences differ where NCBI's do. The database is read in blocks of about 1 MiB; only the sequences of the hit lists are read again, one at a time. Totals above 2^31 letters are kept in 64 bits as NCBI keeps them (`-subject` keeps its limit). Added `blastn -dbsize N` for `-db` and `-subject`; `-searchsp`, `-db_soft_mask` and `-db_hard_mask` stay unsupported.
- `blastn` (all tasks) at one thread hands each subject's HSPs to the hit lists as soon as the subject is searched, as NCBI BLAST+ updates its hit lists one subject at a time, instead of holding the HSPs of every subject of a query batch until all are searched. Reports do not change. Megablast of 100 16S queries against 28,036 16S sequences (40.6 Mb) at one thread: peak memory 237 to 175 MiB (same-run measurement). With two or more threads the same is on by default (`LOSAT_X_BNSTREAM`, one of the switches of the strict set below; `LOSAT_X_BNSTREAM=0` turns it off): the threads search at most 64 subjects per thread ahead of the subject whose HSPs go to the hit lists next, while one of them hands the HSPs over in subject order; on the same search on a 2-socket Xeon workstation, 202 to 141 MiB at 0.94 times the time at 8 threads and 211 to 145 MiB at 0.88 times at 16 threads ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md)).
- Added `blastp -db NAME`: BLASTP searches an NCBI BLAST protein database (the files of makeblastdb without `-parse_seqids`, or of `LOSAT makedb`; format versions 4 and 5, several volumes, alias files with `TITLE` and `DBLIST`, and with `NSEQ` and `LENGTH` when they equal the sums of the listed databases, as `blastdb_aliastool -dblist` writes them; the database is found along NCBI's search path, `BLASTDB` and `.ncbirc`) and gives the output of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `blastp -db NAME -num_threads 1` for outfmt 0, 6 and 7, the same at every `-num_threads`. The database is read in blocks, so the search holds its index (12 bytes per sequence) but not its sequences. `-db` cannot be combined with `-subject` or `-subject_loc`. Rejected explicitly: a database sequence that holds a gap character (`-`), a sequence with more than one definition line, and what the database reader rejects (parsed Seq-ids, id and taxonomy filters, several databases in one name). Added `blastp -dbsize N` (NCBI's effective database length) for `-db` and `-subject` searches; `-searchsp` stays unsupported.
- Added `LOSAT makedb -in FASTA -dbtype prot|nucl -out NAME [-title T] [-max_file_sz S]`: it writes an NCBI BLAST database (format version 4, as `makeblastdb -blastdb_version 4` without `-parse_seqids`) that NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 searches with the same output as on makeblastdb's own database. The sequence and header files are the same bytes as makeblastdb's and the index file differs only in the creation date. `-parse_seqids`, mask and taxonomy options and `-blastdb_version 5` are rejected by name. LOSAT's programs do not search a database yet (`-db` stays a rejection).
- Fixed when `blastp` ends the composition-based redo of a query early. NCBI BLAST+ ends the remaining matches of a query only when the hit lists of all queries of its query batch (10,000 residues of queries) are full. LOSAT looked at the query's own hit list alone with two or more threads and two or more queries that have hits (a query whose hit list was full got the result of searching it alone: two queries against Swiss-Prot differed in 42 of 624 rows), and at the hit lists of all queries of the input, not of the batch, with one thread or one query that has hits. With `-max_target_seqs` 500 either case needs more than 500 significant subjects for one query. The result now follows NCBI's batches at every thread count.
- Fixed the order of two `blastp` HSPs of one query-subject pair whose scores tie only after the composition-based redo rounds them. NCBI BLAST+ sorts each subject's HSPs by E-value when it makes the alignments it reports, and E-values below 1.0e-180 count as equal there, so such a pair is ordered by its rounded score and then by subject start; LOSAT kept the order of the unrounded scores. In 64 OrthoFinder-style jobs (about 600,000 rows) two pairs differed, in order only.
- The exact performance paths of the 2026-10 performance work (the `LOSAT_X_*` switches of the "strict set") are on by default in every program, natively and in WebAssembly. Each of them gives the reference result by construction, so reports do not change. `LOSAT_X_DEFAULT=0` runs the previous paths, and `LOSAT_X_<NAME>=0` turns one switch off ([`docs/agents/diagnostics.md`](docs/agents/diagnostics.md), "Performance switches"). The Web reactors receive no environment and always run the default.
- Costs of the default: megablast keeps one byte per base of a subject while it is searched, and tblastn keeps up to 256 MiB of subject translations for a search of four or more query batches (20,000 query residues per batch); a search of up to three batches keeps none. TBLASTX builds only the lookup tables its seed stage scans, frees them before the HSP list of the last subject of a query batch is merged and linked, and sorts and links the HSP list of a subject without a second copy; on a 0.66 Mb by 0.61 Mb pair its peak memory is 163 MiB at 8 threads and 111 MiB at 1 thread, below the previous paths (320 and 249 MiB in the same measurement). On targets other than x86_64, aarch64 and WebAssembly no vector X-drop kernel exists, and the scalar code runs there.
- TBLASTX gives each thread of a search the next subject when it is free (`LOSAT_X_TBXNEXT`) and makes a subject's frames only while the search reads them (`LOSAT_X_TBXFRAMES`); both are now among the switches above, so reports do not change. Before, rayon cut the subjects into pieces before the search, and the long subjects a genome lists first (its chromosome arms) were searched one after another on one thread. On the workstation (medians of alternating pairs, every report equal to NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 `-db -num_threads 1`) 3 fruit fly transcripts against the fruit fly genome database (1,870 sequences) took 9.75 s at one thread (11.59 s before; NCBI 10.05 s) with a peak memory of 107 MiB (257 MiB; NCBI 162 MiB), and 2.58 s at 8 threads (11.48 s; NCBI 2.73 s) with 316 MiB (257 MiB; NCBI 359 MiB). `LOSAT_X_TBXNEXT=0` and `LOSAT_X_TBXFRAMES=0` turn them off.
- TBLASTX holds less memory in its seed stage (`LOSAT_X_SEEDCOMPACT`, now one of the switches above, so reports do not change): a unit of the parallel seed stage keeps its saved ungapped hits as 28-byte records, the shape NCBI BLAST+ keeps them in, copied out at their exact length, and the merge makes each chunk's list at its exact length and moves the first one into the frame's list instead of copying it. On the workstation (8 rounds, every report equal to NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0), max RSS went from 109 to 87 MiB at one thread and from 157 to 148 MiB at 8 threads on the 0.66 Mb by 0.61 Mb pair (AP027131 x AP027133, genetic code 4), and from 130 to 121 MiB and from 215 to 209 MiB on a genome against itself (NZ_CP006932); the whole search took x0.97-0.99 of the time. `LOSAT_X_SEEDCOMPACT=0` turns it off.
- `tblastn` with two or more threads on many subjects hands each thread a chunk of consecutive subjects at a time instead of one subject, cut by residues as NCBI's database reader cuts them for its search threads: at most `min(2^30, F) / (4 * threads) + 1` bases, at least one subject, where F is a quarter of the subjects' bases (the `.nsq` file `makeblastdb` would write), so a long subject is a chunk of its own (`LOSAT_X_TBNOIDCHUNK`, one of the switches above). On 28,036 16S subjects (40.6 Mb) at 8 threads a 55-protein search took a median of 1.35 s against 2.81 s without it, and a one-protein search 0.61 s against 1.50 s; 20 zebrafish proteins against the fly genome (1,870 records, the chromosome arms first) took 7.0 s against 6.8 s (four rounds on a loaded machine). Reports and peak memory are those of the search without it.
- Megablast's preliminary greedy alignment runs the fused loop by default (`LOSAT_X_GREEDYFUSE`, one of the switches above). Measured in #171 on 100 16S queries against RefSeq 16S (28,036 subjects), one thread, on the workstation: wall time 0.74-0.81 of the reference; NCBI 2.17.0 `-db` over LOSAT 1.24 -> 1.63; the same bytes.
- `tblastx` with two or more threads sorts the index orders of the HSP list of a subject faster (`LOSAT_X_LINKSORT`, one of the switches above): the linking call sorts its output order once where it sorted twice, and a long index sort runs as a stable parallel sort on the search pool, with the same order as before. At 8 threads the whole search took x0.926 of the time on the 0.66 Mb by 0.61 Mb pair (AP027131 x AP027133, genetic code 4) and x0.956 on a genome against itself (NZ_CP006932); at 1 thread the difference is within the noise. Reports and peak memory are unchanged.
- On aarch64 (Linux arm64, macOS on Apple silicon) the X-drop gapped alignment of `blastn -task blastn` and of the protein programs runs in 128-bit NEON vectors by default (`LOSAT_X_DPNEON`, one of the switches above), with the kernel bodies x86_64 and WebAssembly run; before, aarch64 ran the scalar code. On the GitHub Linux arm64 runner (ubuntu-24.04-arm), one thread, median of 7 rounds: `blastn -task blastn` AP027202 x LC738875 10.38 s -> 3.50 s and AP027152 x AP027202 0.201 s -> 0.105 s, `blastp` WSSV x PajaWSV 2.04 s -> 1.25 s, `tblastn` 0.81 s -> 0.67 s, `blastx` 0.86 s -> 0.70 s; megablast does not use this alignment and is unchanged. Reports are the same bytes.
- On aarch64, `blastp` and `tblastn` solve the Newton problems of the composition adjustment eight per round instead of four (`LOSAT_X_NEWTONLANES8`, one of the switches above); each problem keeps its operations, so reports do not change. Measured on the GitHub runners with bench5's blastp_wssv / tblastn_small at one thread: wall time 0.98 / 0.96 of four lanes on Linux arm64 (Neoverse-N2) and 0.97 / 0.89 on macOS arm64. x86-64 and WebAssembly keep four lanes.
- `tblastn` does less work per subject (`LOSAT_X_TBNSTATE` and `LOSAT_X_TBNXLATE`, two of the switches above): a search thread keeps the scan state of its word finder and gapped alignment from one subject to the next, as NCBI's search threads do, instead of making it for each subject and clearing its diagonal table for each piece of a subject (it keeps one state, about 1 MB for 84,000 query residues, until it ends or its next tblastn search), and the six frames of a subject are read from codon tables, as NCBI translates the compressed subject. Reports do not change. On the workstation at 8 threads (alternating rounds; the same reports; peak memory equal or lower): 55 proteins against 28,036 16S sequences (40.6 Mb) 1.07 -> 0.85 s (x0.79; at one thread x0.70), zebrafish proteins against the fly genome 15.8 -> 13.7 s (x0.86), and on a build before #191 fly proteins against the zebrafish transcriptome x0.76, against the human transcriptome x0.74 and against the zebrafish genome x0.87. The 16S search then takes 0.84-0.96 of the time of NCBI BLAST+ 2.17.0 with `-db` at 1, 8, 16 and 32 threads.
- `tblastn` with two or more threads on many subjects no longer waits between entries of chunks and searches the pieces of a long subject on all threads (`LOSAT_X_TBNOIDNEXT` and `LOSAT_X_TBNOIDUNITS`, two of the switches above): a thread takes the next chunk of subjects when it is free, as NCBI's search threads ask the database for the next chunk, and the (frame, piece) units of a subject longer than a chunk's budget (a chromosome) are searched by the threads that have no chunk left, with the results entered in NCBI's order. Reports do not change. On the workstation (alternating rounds; the same reports and CPU time): zebrafish proteins against the fly genome at 8 threads 13.7 -> 9.7 s (x0.71), fly proteins against the zebrafish genome x0.89, against the human genome at 16 threads 272.6 -> 265.4 s (x0.97), against 28,036 16S sequences and the zebrafish transcriptome x1.00 and x1.01.
- `tblastx` links the groups of a subject's HSP list where they lie in the list (`LOSAT_X_LINKSLICES`, one of the switches above): the linking call no longer cuts the sorted list into one list per group and copies the linked groups into a new list, so the list does not exist twice. At 8 threads the whole search took x0.900 of the time on the 0.66 Mb by 0.61 Mb pair (AP027131 x AP027133, genetic code 4), x0.948 on AP027131 against 200 contigs and x0.988 on a genome against itself (NZ_CP006932); at 1 thread the difference is within the noise. Max RSS on NZ_CP006932 against itself went from 141 to 130 MiB at 1 thread and from 235 to 215 MiB at 8 threads; on the other two it is unchanged (on the pair the search peaks in its seed stage). Reports are unchanged.
- `tblastn -subject` holds its subjects packed, about a quarter of a byte per base, instead of their letters (`LOSAT_X_TBNSTORE`, one of the switches above): each record is packed as soon as it is read (with two or more threads by a second thread while the reader goes on), the frames of a subject are translated from the packed bases, as NCBI BLAST+ translates the compressed subject, and the composition-based redo and the report make only the windows of a subject they align. Reports do not change. ## [v0.1.0] - Initial Release Candidate

Initial release candidate for LOSAT as a standalone, pure-Rust reimplementation of NCBI BLAST+ local sequence alignment behavior, designed for direct pairwise FASTA comparisons (`-query` vs `-subject`) with bit-identical output parity and WebAssembly portability.

### Added

- **Supported Programs & Search Tasks**:
  - **`blastn`**: Supports pairwise nucleotide alignment with `megablast` (default, word size 28, affine/linear penalties) and traditional `blastn` (word size 11, match/mismatch 2/-3, gaps 5/2).
  - **`blastp`**: Supports pairwise protein alignment using BLOSUM62 matrix and affine gap penalties (open 11, extend 1).
  - **`tblastx`**: Supports 6-frame translated pairwise nucleotide alignment with full genetic code customization.
- **Bit-Perfect NCBI BLAST+ Parity**: Produces identical alignment boundaries, bit scores, and E-values matching official NCBI BLAST+ (v2.17.0+) across certified pairwise profiles.
- **Standard Output Formats**: Supports canonical tabular output (`-outfmt 6`) and commented tabular (`-outfmt 7`) reporting the standard 12 BLAST fields (`qseqid sseqid pident length mismatch gapopen qstart qend sstart send evalue bitscore`), plus pairwise text alignment (`-outfmt 0`) for `blastp`.
- **Pure-Rust Standalone Runtime**: Zero external C/C++ dependencies or shared libraries. Does not link, embed, wrap, or invoke NCBI BLAST+ binaries at runtime.
- **Direct Pairwise Alignment**: Directly aligns sequences from FASTA files via `-query` and `-subject` without requiring database formatting (`makeblastdb`).
- **WebAssembly & WASI Support**: Compiles to standalone WASI command-line binaries (`wasm32-wasip1`) and multithreaded WASI (`wasm32-wasip1-threads`) for sandboxed CLI and browser-based bioinformatics applications (such as [gbdraw](https://github.com/satoshikawato/gbdraw)).
- **Cross-Platform Native Binaries**: Pre-compiled and verified binaries for Linux (x86_64, aarch64), macOS (Apple Silicon arm64 & Intel x86_64), and Windows (x86_64).
- **Bioconda Distribution**: Officially available on Bioconda as `losat` (`conda install -c bioconda losat`) across Linux and macOS architectures.

### Changed

- User-facing documentation clarifies that NCBI BLAST+ is used strictly as an independent validation oracle during testing, not as a runtime dependency or fallback path.
- Parameter validation fails fast with explicit error messages when encountering unsupported options rather than silently degrading or falling back.
- Wasm documentation clearly distinguishes standalone WASI command builds from experimental browser library APIs.

### Approved Parity Enhancement

- **TBLASTX Subject Genetic Code (`--db-gencode`)**: In local pairwise `-subject` searches, LOSAT explicitly honors non-default `--db-gencode` for translating subject sequences (supporting genetic codes 1–33). This resolves a recognized NCBI BLAST+ limitation where local `-subject` mode defaulted to genetic code 1 regardless of `--db-gencode`. All scoring, statistical calculations, and alignment logic remain strictly identical to NCBI.

### Scope & Known Limitations

- **Database Search**: Pre-formatted BLAST databases (e.g., `.nin`, `.nhr` created by `makeblastdb`) and remote NCBI queries (`-remote`) are outside the v0.1.0 scope; comparisons are strictly pairwise FASTA (`-query` vs `-subject`).
- **Unimplemented Programs**: `blastx` and `tblastn` are not implemented in this release.
- **Discontinuous MegaBLAST**: `dc-megablast` is not yet supported.
- **Library API**: Rust crate library interfaces and embeddable Web/Wasm APIs are currently internal-only; stability is guaranteed at the CLI level.

### Verification Authority

Governed by program certification records in `docs/release/` (`blastn_v0.1.0_certification.md`, `blastp_v0.1.0_certification.md`, `tblastx_v0.1.0_certification.md`) and the pure-Rust runtime authority in `docs/release/pure_rust_runtime_v0.1.0_certification.md`.
